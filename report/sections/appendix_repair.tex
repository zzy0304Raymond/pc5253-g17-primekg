\section{Repair under a Budget (Exploratory)}
\label{sec:repair}

On the sample we removed 30\% or 50\% of the edges at random (ten orders) and let
four policies restore 5\%, 10\% or 20\% of the deleted edges: random repair;
edge betweenness in the intact graph (structural, no labels); relation type,
which restores only drug--protein and disease--protein edges (layer-aware); and a
task-aware score counting the broken two-hop routes of training queries that an
edge alone would complete, which saturates after 31--87 edges. Gains were
measured on 60 held-out queries (about 27 effective ones; 19 of their 21 drugs
also appear in training). Random and relation-type gains follow from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:r2} by changing the edge retention, so they check consistency
rather than test a model; eleven of twelve lie slightly above the measured
gains, by at most 1.5 standard errors. As expected from the layers each policy
restores, relation-type repair is best for two-hop retrieval at a 20\% budget
(it beats edge betweenness by 6.5 points at $f=0.5$, $P=0.002$, significant after
a Bonferroni correction for 24 comparisons), while edge betweenness is best or
tied at $L=3$ and at small budgets (11.0 points at $L=3$, 5\% budget,
$P=0.001$) and recovers all of the giant component's 1.8 points of headroom by
a 10\% budget.
